Pynchon quotation annotation manual

Your task is to identify every marked direct quotation of at least five words from Thomas Pynchon's fiction in each assigned scholarly article, and to confirm the Pynchon work from which it comes.

Starting the task: double-click your launcher—Open Author Annotation or Open Second Annotator. A small black window opens (Terminal on a Mac, Command Prompt on Windows); it starts the private local application, and the annotation dashboard then opens in your browser. You do not need to type anything in that black window; simply leave it open while you work. The documents assigned to you for the current stage are the ones that appear; any later batch will be sent to you separately.

1. What counts

Include text marked by quotation marks or distinct block formatting when it reproduces at least five words from:

  • the novels V., The Crying of Lot 49, Gravity's Rainbow, Vineland, Mason & Dixon, Against the Day, Inherent Vice, Bleeding Edge, and Shadow Ticket;
  • “Mortality and Mercy in Vienna”;
  • the Slow Learner stories “The Small Rain,” “Low-lands,” “Entropy,” “Under the Rose,” and “The Secret Integration.”

Small changes by the scholar do not put a quotation out of scope. Include direct quotations with ellipses, editorial brackets, changed punctuation or capitalisation, minor wording differences, transcription damage, or edition differences. Record those changes in the Alterations field.

2. What does not count

  • quotations from critics, theorists, historians, or other literary authors;
  • Pynchon's nonfiction, including essays, liner notes, and the Slow Learner Introduction;
  • titles, bibliographic references, terminology, or scare quotes;
  • paraphrase, summary, allusion, or other unmarked resemblance;
  • spans shorter than five words.

3. Reviewing each suggested quotation

The application shows a highlighted annotation with surrounding article context. Automatically suggested annotations are aids, not answers.

FieldWhat to do
DecisionChoose Include only for a marked direct quotation from eligible Pynchon fiction. Otherwise choose Exclude.
Source workFor an included quotation, choose the Pynchon work. Use Search Pynchon's texts for this quotation if helpful — but the suggestions are just search results: the app's best guesses, and they can be wrong. Only click Confirm on a suggestion after you have checked it yourself against the quotation.
Chapter/episodeFor an included quotation, confirm which chapter or episode it comes from. This is a click, not a lookup: each source suggestion has a Confirm this source and chapter/episode button that fills in the work and the chapter/episode number together. Check the suggested number against the quotation and its context before accepting it; you can still change it, or type a number directly if you resolve the source another way.
Exclusion reasonFor an excluded annotation, choose the most specific explanation.
Quotation formRecord quotation marks, block quotation, or another marked form.
AlterationsRecord ellipses, brackets, case, punctuation, wording, or other changes, separated by commas.

Choose Save decision and continue. Every save is written immediately, so it is safe to stop and resume later.

4. Published PDF and lost formatting

Use Open published PDF whenever the plaintext does not reveal whether wording was marked as a quotation. Marking is judged from the published article, not from the plaintext conversion.

Block quotations keep their formatting in the plaintext, so what you see in the app matches the published marking; consult the PDF whenever anything looks unclear, and set each in-scope span's form (block or quotation marks).
If a quotation is genuinely Pynchon's fiction but cannot be located in the ten primary texts (for example a variant edition), choose "In scope — NOT locatable in the ten primary texts" in the source dropdown and explain in the notes — the note is required before the document can be marked complete. This differs from "Unresolved / decide later", which means "findable, but not yet confirmed".
Alterations by scholar (in-scope spans only): record how the quotation as printed differs from Pynchon's canonical text, comparing against the matched source snippet in the source suggestion. Use these comma-separated tags, consistently: ellipsis (text omitted, … or […]); brackets (editorial insertion like "[w]here"); wording (the words differ — misquotation, changed tense, swapped word). Leave the field empty when the quote is verbatim — empty means "unaltered". Do not tag pure typography (curly vs straight quotes, dashes). Anything else: nearest tag, plus an explanation in Notes.
When excluding a span, ignore the source-work and chapter fields entirely — they are cleared automatically when you save; only the exclusion reason is recorded. A genuine Pynchon quotation of fewer than five words has its own exclusion reason — use it rather than "other". Count words naturally: hyphenated compounds and contractions are one word. And for the chapter of an included span: click Confirm on the correct source suggestion, which fills the source and chapter together — you are never expected to look chapters up by hand. If no suggestion is right, leave the chapter blank rather than guessing; unresolved spans are settled later by manual search, by design.
Quotations interrupted by attribution — e.g. “We must go over space,” Olson wrote, “or we wither” (114) — appear as separate marked spans; decide each span on its own. Choose the exclusion reason by whose words they are first: a non-Pynchon span is excluded as another author/critic regardless of length. Only for genuine Pynchon fiction does the fewer-than-five-words reason apply, and it applies per span — a three-word Pynchon fragment is excluded even when the whole interrupted sentence is longer. Parenthetical page numbers like (114) are never part of a quotation.

5. Correcting boundaries

If an annotation includes too much or too little text, choose Correct boundaries using the full article. Highlight the complete quotation, choose Use my highlighted text, and save the replacement.

6. Finding quotations the automatic suggestions missed

The automatic suggestions only see text inside quotation marks, and their handling of straight "typewriter-style" single quotes is deliberately cautious. Block quotations — indented passages with no quotation marks at all — are completely invisible to them, so those are always found by reading. This step is required for every article. Do it while you are fresh — before the suggested annotations, part-way through, or after: the order is yours.

  1. Finish deciding all the suggested annotations first.
  2. Choose Read full article and read the whole article in that view. Everything already recorded — suggested annotations and your own additions — appears with a green highlight. Keep Open published PDF open beside it to check anything that looks unclear in the plain text.
  3. You are looking for quotation-like text with no highlight — above all, indented block quotations. When you find one, select the quoted text with your mouse: just the quotation itself, not the surrounding sentence.
  4. Click Use my highlighted text. Your selection appears in the text box.
  5. Pick its form from the dropdown (block quotation / single quotation marks / other) and click Add selected text as a missed quotation.
  6. The new span then opens like any other annotation: make the normal include/exclude decision, confirm the source, and record any alterations.
  7. Return to the full article and keep reading from where you left off (browser search, Command-F on a Mac or Ctrl-F on Windows, helps you jump back).

7. Uncertainty and independence

If the source or its chapter/episode is uncertain, leave it unresolved temporarily and explain the issue in Notes.

If you’d like to double check a quotation, all of Pynchon’s texts are available as .txt files in the Pynchon_primary_texts folder. Open one in a text editor and use its search (Ctrl-F / Command-F).

Independent annotators must not view or discuss one another’s decisions until both copies are complete. Disagreements are expected and will be handled later by the project owner.

8. Completing a document

Mark a document complete only after:

  1. every annotation has an include/exclude decision;
  2. every exclusion has a reason;
  3. every included quotation has a confirmed source work and chapter/episode;
  4. you have read the complete article and added any omissions.

The application checks these requirements automatically. When you want to stop, choose Finish for now at the top right and then close the browser tab. Previously saved work will reappear at the next launch.

The application runs only on this computer, uses no internet service, and does not send article text or annotations elsewhere.
